What is SP 800-63:
SP 800-63(version 4 now) is a set of guidelines which speaks about digital identity management and how to chose assurance levels so we can match the strength of checks to the risk of the system. Identity Assurance Level - speaks about confirming the user is the same as who they are in real life. can be verified by their license, SSC. Authenticator Assurance level - speaks about confirming that the person who registered is logging in. Confirmed by MFA, passwords. Federation Assurance Level: confirms or confides regarding how identity attributesa and assertions are transmitted across systems.
Why would a non- US company adopt this: 1. It gives their target audience confidence that they are following a benchmark. Also the SP 800-63 is being widely used by companies across the globe because they have a solid set of guidelines like listed above. It can be converted into controls. Its mandatory for US federal agencies.

AuthN vs AuthZ: which is more common?
AuthN failures with strong AuthZ are basically weak passwords or shared passwords for accounts. They could be admin accounts or accounts with most access to the systems. What weak authN does is give access to people who are not supposed to reach the secure data. let's say an admin account is shared by a FEDev/ Marketing Analyst and a System Admin. The marketing analyst should not have access to the database which is now enabled by a shared admin account. It also does not help in audit trails becuase now we only know that the admin account was used but we don't know "who" used it.
In case of AuthN being strong and AuthZ being weak: the problem is that any authenticated user will have access to all the detailed. This could be an sales rep having access to deleting a plan which is least sold.
So it all depends on which lens you are looking at. it's either a breach of credential or a excess priviledge which is noticed.

employee lifecycle:
Joiner -> Mover -> Leaver:
I strongly feel that the mover is the most underestimated move. Let's say a sales rep becomes a developer tomorrow. Initially they had access to the sales dashboard where they could log entries of the sales made, revenue which came in and the billing details of the customer.
Now the software engineer which is the new role of this user has no business in knowing the billing details of the customer. They are a junior developer and have no access to the database(production) but their old permission as sales rep is giving them access to the user's billing and credit card details. Nobody would notice because "nothing breaks". The following control would catch this over priviledge: Period review of access and catalog match. The role of the user and the permissions for that user should match. if it doesn't its a red flag.
